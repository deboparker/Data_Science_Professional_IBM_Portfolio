## 📌 Overview
This project practices data manipulation and labelled visualization in Jupyter Notebooks, using a dataset of country-level happiness scores and average income.

## 📊 The Graph

![Happiness vs Income Scatter Plot](./images/happiness_scatter.png)

- **X-axis:** Average Income *(independent variable)*
- **Y-axis:** Happiness Score *(dependent variable)*

## 🏷️ Data Points of Interest
Labelled points started as individual countries in the early graphs. I then filtered the data by region, which produced 10 distinct regions, and added a second filter comparing collective vs. non-collective cultures.

## 🔍 Explanation

**Why I chose these columns, and how I filtered the data:**
The first graph plots average income against happiness score. On its own it didn't reveal much, since it included too many countries at once. I filtered the data into 10 regions to check whether happiness differed by region — theorizing that collective cultures would score higher on happiness, while non-collective cultures would show higher income. I then labelled the data points with average happiness scores to look for a correlation between happiness and income. The scatterplot shows a positive correlation between the two.

**What makes the labelled points interesting:**
Western Europe, North America, and Australia/New Zealand are all non-collective cultures, and they had the highest average happiness scores in the labelled bar graphs. This supports the theory that non-collective cultures have higher income *and* a correlation between happiness and income — though it isn't statistically proven, since the scatter plot doesn't show a perfect line. Investigating cultural variation within each region would be the next step, to identify which countries are outliers within their region.
